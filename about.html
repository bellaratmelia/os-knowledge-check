About the Open Scholarship Knowledge Check

What this is

The Open Scholarship Knowledge Check is a low-stakes self-assessment for exploring your understanding of open scholarship, scholarly communication, and research infrastructure.

It is designed primarily for librarians and research support professionals, but may also be useful to researchers, infrastructure providers, technologists, and others working in this area. The aim is to help you identify what you already know, uncover gaps in your understanding, and discover reliable resources for further learning.

The question bank can also be used as a teaching resource. Educators, librarians, and trainers are welcome to draw on it for workshops, classroom activities, team discussions, or informal professional development.

This is a learning tool, not a certification or comprehensive assessment of professional expertise. You can use it as often as you like, and nothing you do here is recorded.

Why this exists

There is no shortage of well-curated open scholarship material to read. Primers, guides, LibGuides, curated link lists, training modules, and community glossaries cover the introductory layer well, and much of that material is excellent. What I think is missing is a way to check whether any of it stuck.

Reading a clear explanation of the difference between green and gold open access can feel like understanding it, but being asked to explain that difference from memory is a different exercise, and often a more revealing one. This gap between recognising an answer and being able to recall it is one reason the knowledge check includes both multiple-choice and short-answer modes.

The scope of open scholarship also presents a challenge. Librarians and research support professionals may be expected to advise on identifiers, licensing, publishing models, funder policies, repositories, research assessment, integrity, research data, reproducibility, and infrastructure. The details change regularly, and there are relatively few low-stakes opportunities to identify areas that may need refreshing before they arise in a consultation, workshop, or project.

In this site, getting an answer wrong here costs nothing. Instead, each question is an opportunity to review an explanation, revisit a concept, and find a resource for further learning.

About the creator

I developed and maintain this project independently. It does not represent an institutional assessment, endorsement, or certification.

How it works

  1. Choose an answer format, one difficulty level, and one or more topic areas.
  2. Answer the matching questions one at a time.
    • In multiple-choice mode, choose the best answer from five options. Your answers are scored automatically.
    • In short-answer mode, write an answer from memory, compare it with the model answer, and grade your own response. This format tests recall rather than recognition.
  3. After each response, review the correct answer, an explanation, and, where available, a link to authoritative further reading.
  4. At the end, view your score and, if you selected more than one topic area, a breakdown by area.
Note

Questions are selected randomly from those matching your chosen level and topic areas. A score therefore reflects your performance on the questions included in that session. It should not be interpreted as a certification or complete measure of professional knowledge.

Difficulty levels

Level Focus
Beginner Foundational concepts, definitions, and terminology.
Intermediate How concepts work, how they are applied, and how they connect.
Expert Nuanced distinctions, standards, policies, and comparisons between related tools or approaches.

Difficulty describes the complexity of the questions, not the professional status of the person answering them.

Topic areas

  • Open Access: Principles, publishing models, licences, policies, repositories, and economic arrangements that shape access to and reuse of scholarly publications.

  • Preprints & Peer Review: Early sharing of research and the processes, models, and platforms used to evaluate, review, certify, and curate scholarly work.

  • Persistent Identifiers & Metadata: Identifiers and metadata that distinguish researchers, organisations, outputs, projects, and other research entities, and connect them reliably across scholarly systems.

  • Open Scholarly Infrastructure: Services, standards, platforms, organisations, and governance arrangements that support the creation, discovery, connection, access, and preservation of scholarly knowledge.

  • Research Data Management: Planning, organising, documenting, storing, protecting, sharing, preserving, and governing research data throughout and beyond the research lifecycle.

  • Research Software: Practices for developing, licensing, documenting, sharing, citing, reviewing, preserving, and sustaining software created or used in research.

  • Reproducibility & Open Methods: Practices that make research plans, methods, materials, analyses, and computational environments transparent, assessable, and reproducible.

  • Research Assessment & Metrics: Quantitative indicators, qualitative approaches, and reform principles used to evaluate research outputs, contributions, researchers, and institutions.

  • Research Integrity & Ethics: Principles and practices that support responsible research and publication, including ethical oversight, authorship, disclosure, prevention of misconduct, and correction of the scholarly record.

Using it for teaching

The knowledge check can be used individually or incorporated into teaching and professional development. For example, facilitators might use selected questions to begin a discussion, check understanding during a workshop, revisit concepts after training, or identify topics that a team would like to explore further.

Questions can be filtered by topic area and difficulty level, making it possible to create an activity suited to a particular audience. Because questions include explanations and links to further reading, they can also serve as starting points for discussion rather than being used only for scoring.

Privacy

There is no login, analytics, or storage of your answers. Your selections and responses remain in your current browser session. Refreshing the page resets the quiz.
